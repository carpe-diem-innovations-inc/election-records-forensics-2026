Election Records Forensics 2026

An open method and dataset for examining the four document archives published at whitehouse.gov/election-integrity (released 16 July 2026): the files' metadata and structure, the integrity of their redactions, the text of the underlying memos, and how the claims compare to the public record.

Don't trust this. Test your own reality. Every finding is re-derivable from the official source files. Reproducibility is anchored to SHA-256 hashes — if your re-download matches, you are analyzing the exact same bytes. One command re-derives it all: python scripts/trust_but_verify.py — and a weekly CI run repeats it in public.

What it is — and isn't

  • Is: a neutral, non-partisan, source-anchored verification kit; a worked example of document forensics.
  • Isn't: an accusation or a claim that any election was altered. Interpretation is labeled as interpretation.

How to read the findings

  • Tier 1 — Reproducible fact. Re-derivable with the scripts.
  • Tier 2 — Sourced fact-check. Claims vs. the public record.
  • Tier 3 — Interpretation. Fenced; ships with a steelman and a falsification test.
