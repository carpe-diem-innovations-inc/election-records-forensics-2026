04

Claims vs. the public record

The metadata shows how the package was built. This tests what it asserts. "Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence." — Carl Sagan

TIER 2 · SOURCED

China “compromised” 200–220M voter records
 MISLEADING Commercial voter files (full files ≈200M records, marketed as covering nearly every U.S. adult) are compiled from largely public records and sold openly in 32 states + DC. Acquiring such data ≠ hacking vote counts. Pew, Ballotpedia.

05

What the intelligence actually says

The 16 scanned memos were OCR'd. Their own words settle the central question.

TIER 1 · REPRODUCIBLE

Reading the memos directly converts inference into a primary-source finding: every substantive document describes data collection and social-media influence operations. None asserts that a voter roll was altered, a ballot changed, or a tabulation compromised. An altered-election claim appears nowhere in the memo text.

“Summary” framing memos

China's plans were “to sway public opinion against the Trump Administration,” “exploit U.S. societal fissures,” projected “into social media (TikTok, Facebook, Twitter, YouTube)… overt and hidden influencers.” → influence ops

“18 States” & “200M Records”

China obtained voter data for “person matching and public opinion analysis.” The “200M” is a single 2016 leaked dataset (204,822,241 records) on a PRC list on which “most of the entries were targets in other countries.” → data theft, not a breach of vote systems

The hawkish China memo

Stamped “ALTERNATIVE ANALYSIS.” Carries the IC mainline: “Beijing has not deployed influence efforts intended to change the outcome.” Dissent = “low-level, exploratory steps,” low-to-medium confidence.

FBI's own 2020 objection

FBI Counterintelligence called specific assertions in the elevated minority view “extremely misleading” and “highly misleading and a misrepresentation of the underlying reports,” and wrote that citing “the absence of evidence” is “an absolute red line” for the FBI. Director Wray had signed the mainline ICA.

Who released it

Across dozens of pages the release stamp is COUNSEL TO THE PRESIDENT WARRINGTON · 10 JUL 2026 (variously “declassified by” / “approved for public release by”) — a single White House Counsel's office release pass. Some documents also carry agency stamps (the Venezuela note: “Declassified by D/CIA Ratcliffe on 1 July 2026”; the NICM memo: a Presidential declassification stamp of 3 July 2026). (Names in these stamps are OCR-read; the authority-and-date pattern is what is consistent.)

All 25 Michigan PDFs OCR'd (re-derive locally; not republished). A real paid-canvasser fraud — caught, then declined on the merits.

The real pattern

Canvassers were paid per registration (loaded on prepaid cards) with quotas "to get paid" — the textbook incentive to pad. That is application fraud for pay, caught before processing — not fraudulent ballots or altered votes.

The framing gap

The filename "Gift Cards Suppress Trump" is curator-supplied: the underlying witness interview contains no admission supporting it (re-derive it from the source archive; witness-interview text is deliberately not republished here). Several curator-supplied filenames assert an intent the interview texts do not contain.

The "4-country graphic" email (Sept 2020) shows CIA, FBI, and INR urging caution against elevating a "preference line," because consensus was that China judged election interference "not worth the risk." It records a multi-agency dispute over messaging, not vote manipulation — the same dispute as the ICA minority view, captured live.
07

What the corpus does & doesn’t support

Reading only what the documents can actually carry.

TIER 1 · REPRODUCIBLE
Supported by the evidence
  • Foreign interest in, and collection of, U.S. voter-registration data is real and long-standing.
  • Election software and local-government networks carry genuine, documented vulnerabilities.
  • A real 2020 Michigan fraudulent-application case existed — and was caught by officials.
  • The declassified files were redacted competently.
Not supported by this release
  • That any 2020 or 2024 vote count or election outcome was altered.
  • That “200M compromised records” means hacked machines rather than largely public data.
  • That noncitizen registration exists at outcome-determinative scale.
  • Any revival of the Venezuela / Dominion machine theory.
Bottom line (reproducible). The underlying memos describe data collection and social-media influence operations; none asserts that a voter roll, ballot, or tabulation was altered — and the most forward-leaning memo itself carries the IC mainline judgment that Beijing has "not deployed influence efforts intended to change the outcome."
TIER 3 · INTERPRETATION — NOT ASSERTED AS FACT

One reading: the release couples genuinely-declassified intelligence with narrative documents that supply framing the raw files don't establish — the gap between what the framing invites readers to conclude and what the documents state is itself measurable from the primary text — real espionage material acting as a credibility carrier, landing amid live litigation over voter-data initiatives and ahead of the pre-election quiet period.

The competing reading (steelman): a good-faith effort to warn states and harden election systems — supported by the sober, measured CISA report and the footnoted threat overview included in the same package.

Falsification tests: had the release omitted the court-rejected Venezuela theory and partisan phrasing, the good-faith reading would dominate; had it led with the IC mainline rather than the minority view, a "suppression" framing would be unsupported; if the timing is unrelated to the litigation/quiet-period calendar, the "calibrated" reading weakens. Weigh them yourself — see FINDINGS.md, Tier 3.
